Person has traveled extensively over the last decade, across a wide range of countries and regions. Of all the countries visited, Person liked Portugal and Peru the best, for their food and culture. Both cultures are old and distinct: Portuguese culture is more relaxed and closely tied to the coast, while Peruvian culture is more mountain-oriented and rooted in a strong sense of tradition. Person is fascinated by how two cultures with long, layered histories, both shaped by periods of colonization, developed such different relationships with their land.

Person has fond memories of living in Morocco, brief as the stay was. Person stayed in a smaller inland town far from the main tourist areas, which allowed for direct interaction with the local culture. That town had a strong local textile trade, so Person saw firsthand how the region handles seasonal labor and the differences in how those workers are treated compared with similar labor in the United States. The chance to live somewhere so removed from tourism made the experience memorable.

Another notable trip was time spent in Italy. Person fell in love with Turin, in the northern part of the country, visited originally to see a business contact during an earlier stretch of remote work. Turin is the kind of city Person would love to retire in; its combination of nature, family feeling, and calm but steady energy made the visit especially meaningful. Person was less fond of Venice, where the touristic emphasis and the constant crowds and noise felt overwhelming and exhausting.

Person thoroughly enjoyed traveling through Central America, where each country had its own distinct character. Costa Rica was found to be the most beautiful, with the most opportunity for outdoor adventure, but the local people were seen as somewhat more reserved and more focused on tourism revenue than on genuine interaction. Person holds no strife toward them; it's simply recognized that a more tourism-driven economy creates incentives that make many interactions feel formal or commercial rather than personal.

By contrast, Person found the people of Panama more genuine, whether genuinely interested or genuinely indifferent toward tourists, though scams were more frequent there than in Costa Rica. Both countries had striking natural beauty, but the historical elements of Panama were of greater interest, given the country's canal history and its long relationship with U.S. involvement. Person also found Panamanian food somewhat better than Costa Rican food, while enjoying both a great deal.

Person enjoyed Guatemala the most of all the Central American countries visited, finding it the most vibrant for small business and the most interesting blend of nature and historical sites. Person was particularly fond of Antigua and the way it wove together different cultural influences from its colonial period and the years surrounding it. Person also really loved Guatemala City, a place felt comfortable enough to live and work in remotely, as was the case during an earlier period of remote employment.

Person enjoyed Cuenca in Ecuador, though parts felt overly touristy and much of the stay was spent close to the place being rented. Person hopes to return, having found the region's blend of indigenous and colonial traditions among the most fascinating cultural expressions encountered while traveling. Having never visited a country with a similarly strong indigenous religious tradition before, it prompted conversations that recalled an earlier academic interest in comparative religion. Person also loved that so much of the food was plant-based.

Person thoroughly enjoyed the food throughout Ecuador, where much of it was plant-based and the quality was the highest of any country visited in the region. Across Central and South America, food was a true highlight of the travels: Panamanian cuisine was found somewhat better than Costa Rican, yet both were enjoyed thoroughly, and Ecuadorian food was rated at the very top. For Person, the distinct culinary identity of each country visited was one of the most rewarding parts of exploring the region as a whole.

Person has a deep love for Peru, having lived there for an extended period and traveled to most parts of the country. Stops included Lima, Cusco, Arequipa, Trujillo, Piura, Chiclayo, Iquitos, Puno, Ayacucho, Huaraz, Tacna, Cajamarca, Huancayo, Tarapoto, and several small towns in the highlands, as well as Puerto Maldonado. Person would love to travel to more of Peru but likely won't in the near future. Person picked up intermediate spoken Spanish, communicating in both business and personal settings.

Among everything Person ate in Peru, a distinct fondness developed for coastal cuisine, especially seafood dishes of every kind, both raw and cooked. Person particularly likes the ceviche common on the streets of Lima. The extended time living in the country, combined with intermediate spoken Spanish, allowed for exploring its food well beyond what most visitors ever reach, and coastal Peruvian cooking remains one of the strongest, most lasting culinary memories from that time.

In Portugal, Person traveled to several places, including Sintra, Óbidos, and Porto. Person loved bacalhau, caldo verde, francesinha, and pastéis de nata. Of every cuisine ever eaten, Portuguese is probably the favorite; if Person could only eat Portuguese food for the rest of life, it would be more than enough. Portugal, alongside Peru, ranks as one of Person's two favorite countries from a decade of travel, and its remarkable food is a large part of why it left such a lasting impression.

Another thing Person loved about Portugal was the wine. Many people do not know just how old Portuguese winemaking traditions are, but Person does, having enjoyed wine from one of the oldest wine-producing regions in Europe. For Person, drinking Portuguese wine was not just a pleasure but a connection to centuries of history, deepening an appreciation for a country whose food, culture, and traditions already made it one of the very favorite places in the world.

One of the things Person enjoyed most about Porto and Portugal was that, despite some worn buildings, little had been done to destroy the older, beautiful architecture from earlier centuries. This left abundant evidence of what different eras of construction looked like. Person did not find this in other parts of Europe visited, where much older architecture had been torn down for modern buildings; in Portugal, older architecture remained maintained and prevalent.

Person did not get much time in Slovenia or Croatia, but loved Ljubljana, a small but lively city full of delicious food where the people felt welcoming and genuinely curious about Americans, even in the middle of winter. Person found Croatia less exciting overall, though enjoyed laid-back Rovinj and would happily return for a quiet co-working space found there. Visiting off-season, Person took a dip in the Adriatic and cannot imagine ever feeling as cold in water as on that day.
